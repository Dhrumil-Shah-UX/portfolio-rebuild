Key decisions

Let the scene do the explaining.

01 · Sequence the story

One system. Six understandable steps.

I separated energy recovery from heat-pump conditioning and paired each step with a focused camera view.

02 · Preserve engineering accuracy

Recover energy. Keep air streams separate.

Directional paths and lightweight particles make movement visible. Equipment callouts identify the units; cyan cues explain energy transfer without suggesting air mixing.

03 · Profile before simplifying

Reduce the cost of the city. Preserve the rooftop story.

An observed ~1 GB tab-memory spike led me to diagnose the scene. City textures were the dominant estimated cost, so I optimized the surroundings before cutting the flow effects.

Diagnostic finding61

Runtime city texture objects

~843 MiB estimated decoded footprint
Design & engineering response
  • Three shared city materials
  • Optional, progressive city loading
  • Rendering pauses in hidden tabs
What the stability checks showed

Three complete forward/reverse flow cycles stabilized renderer resource counts. Recorded post-exit JavaScript heap samples differed by ~88 KB. These checks support flow stability; they do not establish an exact GPU-memory reduction.

Responsive experience

Make room for the model.

I replaced competing floating controls with a bottom sheet on mobile and tablet. The same sheet handles exploration and System Flow, keeping Previous/Next primary.

Keep the scene visibleImpact details begin collapsed on smaller screens.

Evidence and measurement limits

QA results come from the project report. User comprehension and engagement gains were not measured; a real screen-reader sanity check remained recommended. Engineering figures visible in the screenshots describe the ventilation intervention, not an impact caused by the showroom. The airflow effects are explanatory animations, not a physical simulation.

Reflection

Clarity is a system-wide decision.

Narrative, camera behavior, engineering accuracy, and browser performance had to work together. Next, I would test whether non-engineers can explain energy recovery without air mixing and distinguish the heat pump’s role.
