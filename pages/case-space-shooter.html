Problem

Could a vague idea become something I could actually play?

I wanted a small enough experiment to move through the complete build process. Early conversations explored familiar mechanics, including Flappy Bird-style interaction, before narrowing toward a space rocket game.

Decisions

Explore, structure, build—and review.

01/03Narrow the idea

Choose a manageable direction before implementation.

I used an LLM to compare simple game mechanics. The goal was a small, buildable interaction, not a broad specification with an expanding feature list.

Evidence boundary: the recording demonstrates the game interface. It is not a user test, performance benchmark, or evidence of an autonomous in-game AI system.

02/03Structure the build

Break implementation into reviewable steps.

After selecting a concept, I used an LLM to create a sequence of implementation tasks and completed them in Cursor one at a time. This made it possible to review the evolving prototype throughout development.

03/03Retain direction

Use tools to support decisions, not replace them.

The LLM supported ideation and task structure; Cursor supported implementation. I selected what to build, evaluated the output, and directed each iteration. This experiment informed later AI-assisted prototypes.
