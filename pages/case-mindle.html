Problem

Sharing required effort before members received any value.

Community members had work to share, but open-ended prompts asked them to recall their week, choose a story, and decide whether to make it public. The record notes about two reflections over one month—not an inactive community, but a participation opportunity.

The loop is a product hypothesis. It does not establish that a puzzle causes a sustained sharing habit.

Decisions

Make participation a smaller next step.

01/04Invite · Support

Use a familiar game with optional help.

I chose letter and full-word guessing with 13 attempts, three progressive hints, and configurable prefilled letters. The lesson follows the result, connecting the puzzle to a useful UX concept.

Trade-off: familiar mechanics reduce the need to learn a new game, but vocabulary still affects difficulty. Content-level hints and prefilled letters provide support without changing the rules.

02/04Reflect · Later design direction

Replace two broad questions with one relevant check-in.

The original reflection asked what players built and what challenged them. I selected a four-category check-in so a learner, someone making progress, or someone needing help would have a relevant starting point.

Scope: only the relevant follow-up would appear. The adaptive flow is a documented later decision; its production deployment and effect on response rates are not established here.

03/04Identity · Privacy direction

Separate trying the game from public participation.

I selected an immediate-play direction with identity requested when relevant to sharing. The reflection design offered named, anonymous, and private responses, with the consequences disclosed before submission.

Trade-offs: browser identity can be lost across devices or storage resets. Private contributions do not become community content. A proposed reflection streak would count every visibility choice—not reward agreement to public sharing.

04/04Operate · Measure

Design the weekly operation alongside the player flow.

I built an admin workflow for words, hints, lessons, prefilled letters, and timezone-aware scheduling. A later six-event funnel was selected to locate drop-off without collecting every click.

Technical contribution: the record documents Server Actions, Supabase persistence, signed gameplay state, and server-side admin secrets. The reflection schema direction preserves older responses. A Friday roundup strategy connects last week’s lesson, community highlights, and the next puzzle without spoilers.

Impact

A production core, with early signals worth investigating.

The record supports a shipped game, persistent results/reflections, admin content scheduling, and Vercel deployment. It does not establish a causal engagement lift.

Early reflection signal
2 reflections over approximately one month before launch; 3 over the first two weeks afterward. Different windows and a small sample prevent a causal or sustained-habit claim.
July 6, 2026 snapshot
7 non-seed registered players and 6 scheduled concepts. Earlier test-player deletion also removed related results, so retained rows are not lifetime completions.
What remains to validate
Adaptive reflection, sharing controls, browser identity, streak behavior, and the proposed event funnel need deployment verification and follow-up evaluation. No WCAG conformance is claimed.

The next question is what happens after the puzzle.

I want to understand contribution quality and whether reflections lead to useful support or conversation. Completion alone cannot answer that.
