Challenge

Core learning flows needed to feel clearer without making the app more complex.

The app served learners across different age groups, but several core flows created friction through unclear feedback, dense layouts, inconsistent navigation, and interaction patterns that were difficult to understand.

What shaped my thinking

Mentor feedback shifted the project from adding UI to improving usability.

01

I learned to distinguish feature expansion from usability improvement and focused the redesign on clearer core tasks.

02

Core actions needed clearer feedback.

Field states, errors, touch targets, labels, and navigation cues needed to make the interface easier to recover from.

03

The redesign had to fit Android patterns.

I used Material Design guidance and implementation constraints to keep the proposed screens realistic for Android.

Key decisions

Four decisions show how the redesign became simpler, clearer, and more implementable.

Decision 01

Make sign-up and login easier to recover from.

Challenge: Login and sign-up needed clearer feedback when users entered incomplete or incorrect information.

My thinking: Recovery should happen close to the field instead of forcing users to interpret a separate modal or remember what went wrong.

Progress: I explored password visibility, visible password hints, stronger field focus, inline errors, spacing, touch interaction, and labels outside fields where useful.

Solution: The final direction made requirements and errors visible in context so users could understand and correct input more easily.

Decision 02

Make enrolled courses easier to return to.

Challenge: Learners needed a clearer path back to enrolled and recent courses without scanning unrelated content first.

My thinking: Navigation should make repeated learning tasks visible and predictable.

Progress: I introduced the My Classes destination in the bottom navigation, moved notifications to the top bar, separated recent courses from enrolled courses, and adjusted scrolling direction to improve organization.

Solution: The navigation direction made returning to learning activity a primary path rather than a buried task.

Decision 03

Reduce visual clutter and clarify hierarchy.

Challenge: Too many visual elements competed for attention across learning and discovery screens.

My thinking: Users should be able to distinguish primary actions, navigational content, informational content, and clickable versus non-clickable areas.

Progress: I refined layouts such as Home, Explore, Course Overview, cards, categories, and tab labels to reduce decorative clutter and clarify what could be acted on.

Solution: The strongest final examples should show fewer competing elements and a clearer path through content.

Decision 04

Remove or simplify UI that looked interactive or confusing.

Challenge: Some interface elements risked looking tappable or visually important even when they were not the main action.

My thinking: Visual emphasis should match user intent. If an element looks interactive, it needs to behave that way or be simplified.

Progress: I reviewed examples such as Course Class and No Internet states for ambiguous iconography, unclear action emphasis, and confusing visual priority.

Solution: The final example should focus on one clear story, such as a clearer Try Again action or a simplified class/progress interface.

Reflection

What I carried forward

I learned that improving a product does not always mean adding features. Mentor feedback pushed me to separate product expansion from usability improvement, simplify interactions, and support my decisions with stronger evidence. Working from Figma into Jetpack Compose also helped me understand how design decisions translate into implementation constraints.
