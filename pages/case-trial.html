My contribution

I designed key steps for entering medical history and led work on guides, FAQs and an assistant concept. I also worked on trial comparison and caregiver permissions, and proposed connection requests between patients.

We reviewed research and physician feedback as a team. I helped group what we learned and turn feedback into design changes.

Problem

Finding a trial starts with the right information

Our brief focused on people whose cancer hadn’t responded to standard treatment. Each study has its own rules for who can join, and trial coordinators need the right medical details to check them.

Share medical history
Answer focused questions about diagnosis and past treatment.
See what joining involves
Make sense of study requirements, travel and repeat visits.
Get help on their terms
Decide what a caregiver can see and who can contact them.

How we researched it We reviewed published research and asked physicians to talk through the prototype as they used it.

01/04Onboarding

Give patients two ways to share their history

In rounds one and three, physicians questioned how much detail we asked for at setup. I worked on the steps for entering medical history. We designed a choice between answering questions and connecting health records so patients could get started with whichever option they were comfortable using.

Earlier exploration

Revised entry point

Travel preferences Patients could also indicate how far they were willing to travel.

02/04Discovery

Call them suggestions

In round two, physicians flagged that “Matching Trials” sounded like we had already checked who could join. We changed the label and kept the doctor’s review visible so patients knew what still needed checking.

03/04Comparison

Put two trials side by side

Switching between study pages makes it easy to lose track of the details. I helped line up study length, visit frequency and who can join so patients could spot differences and bring specific questions to their doctor.

04/04Support throughout the journey

Let patients decide what to share

Physicians raised concerns about unwanted messages and privacy. I proposed asking patients to accept a connection before another patient could message them. I also helped design separate caregiver permissions so patients could ask for help and still decide who could see their records.

What changed after feedback

We renamed the trial list, refined the medical history questions and gave patients more control over sharing. Physicians were still asking about suggestion accuracy after round three.

Patient outcomes

We reviewed a prototype with physicians. With no patient testing or live rollout, we had no data on patient understanding, enrollment or health outcomes.

Where we drew the line

We explored a dashboard for doctors, but it needed more supporting tools. We kept the prototype focused on the patient experience.

I learned how much a single label can promise. I’d next ask patients what a suggestion means to them and what they’d want to ask their doctor.
