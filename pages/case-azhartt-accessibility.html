My contribution

I evaluated the three pages, mapped findings to accessibility criteria, annotated the evidence in Figma, and grouped recurring barriers into component-level recommendations. This is a separate standards-based audit from the five-participant AZHARTT usability project.

Problem

Essential rescue information had barriers beyond visual layout.

Missing labels, invisible focus, and low contrast could make the same page difficult to understand or operate through different access methods. The audit traced these barriers across three service pages.

  1. Understand the pageMeaning was not always available

    Missing image descriptions, inconsistent headings, and unnamed controls affected assistive-technology navigation.

  2. Move through a taskKeyboard access broke down

    The report flagged hover-only menus, absent focus indicators, missing skip links, and a popup keyboard trap.

  3. Read and actImportant cues were hard to perceive

    Low contrast, color-only states, and small targets affected reading and interaction.

A scoped audit, not a disability-user study

The findings describe barriers identified during evaluation. The supplied record does not document testing with disabled participants, a named screen-reader test setup, or a post-remediation validation study.

Decisions

Turn technical findings into fixes a team can act on.

I organized the work around recurring barriers and their consequences for access, rather than treating each screenshot as an isolated defect.

01/04Find the pattern

Fix recurring barriers at the component level.

Missing names, invisible focus, and contrast problems recurred across the reviewed pages. I grouped these into system-level recommendations for navigation, controls, image handling, and typography.

Design judgment: address repeated component behavior first, while keeping page-specific issues traceable. No failures recorded under Understandable in this sample does not establish full coverage or conformance.

02/04Restore control

Keep the keyboard path open.

The report identified hover-only navigation and a Found Pet popup that could trap keyboard users. I prioritized keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, a skip link, and a keyboard-accessible exit.

Priority rationale: an inaccessible exit can block a task entirely. The recommendation is documented; a repaired modal and successful retest are not.

03/04Expose meaning

Expose meaning, not just visual hierarchy.

I recommended descriptive alternatives for informative images, empty alternatives for decorative images, logical headings, and programmatic names for icon-only controls. The annotations connect each issue to a specific part of the page.

Separate appearance from semantics: larger-looking text is not automatically a heading. I made both the observed structure and the proposed hierarchy explicit for implementation.

04/04Support perception and input

Make states and controls easier to perceive.

The audit flagged hero/footer contrast, color-only states, small secondary controls, and missing input-purpose attributes. I recommended stronger contrast, non-color state cues, adequately sized or spaced targets, and appropriate autocomplete metadata.

Targets & input

Scope matters: decorative graphics are not automatically subject to non-text contrast requirements. These recommendations require implementation checks; mockups alone cannot prove conformance.

Impact

A prioritized accessibility roadmap.

The audit chart records 35 failures across the reviewed checks: 24 critical and 11 moderate. These are audit classifications, not resolved issues or measured user-impact gains.

Delivered evidence
Three annotated Figma boards, a cross-page pattern analysis, severity classifications, and prioritized recommendations.
Fix first
Keyboard access and exits, visible focus and skip navigation, meaningful names and image alternatives, heading structure, and contrast.
Fix next
Target sizing/spacing and input-purpose metadata, with affected controls verified in context.
Not established
No verified production remediation, post-fix audit, assistive-technology retest, or measured improvement in rescue-service access is documented.

What I took forward

An accessibility finding becomes actionable when the barrier, affected interaction, criterion, and proposed fix are visible together. Repeated issues also point to shared-component work, not just page-by-page cleanup.
