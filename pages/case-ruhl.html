Challenge

Connect a building search to a useful next step.

My initial direction focused on helping users discover NYC buildings and access relevant building information through a clear, intuitive interface. The UX problem was how to connect property discovery, building details, and an inquiry without losing the context of the selected building.

The work also had a delivery constraint: a stakeholder introduced a different visual and structural direction. I needed to respond to those requirements through substantial redesign and redevelopment.

Design evolution

A change in direction meant rebuilding the experience.

  1. My initial UX directionDevelop a clear property-search experience centered on finding NYC buildings and understanding their information.

The trade-off

Adapt the delivery while keeping ownership clear.

My original UX approach and the requested website direction were different. Moving forward meant revisiting design and development work rather than carrying the initial approach through unchanged.

I was responsible for translating the new requirements into a working website and search-related experience. The revised visual and structural direction was stakeholder-directed; it should not be read as my original design recommendation.

Technical implementation

Design decisions carried through into development.

I worked on property-search functionality using Next.js, Supabase, and Vercel, alongside Wix Studio development. For the stakeholder-directed website, I personally redesigned and developed the landing page and building inquiry form with NYC address search in Wix Studio.

The original application code has not been reviewed for this case study, so I am not assigning specific API, database, hosting, or integration responsibilities to those tools. The verified deliverable is my completed website and search-related work.

Implementation detail

A separately hosted interaction embedded in Wix.

The prototype embeds my GitHub Pages particle demo inside a Wix HTML component. Its source uses a Three.js WebGL canvas, samples text into point positions, and updates the particles in an animation loop. Pointer movement influences their motion.

Interactive detail

A small piece of the website you can try.

I developed a particle treatment for “AI - Enhanced MEP.” The effect combines a text-shaped particle field with subtle motion and pointer interaction.

Its implementation balances movement with keeping the words recognizable: particles are constrained around the text, while capped speed and pixel density limit the animation workload.

Delivery & reflection

Adaptability was part of the product work.

I completed my assigned website and search-related work. The challenge extended beyond designing an interface: I had to respond to changed requirements, revisit earlier decisions, and redevelop the website within a stakeholder-directed brief.

The lesson I carry forward is to make the relationship between user needs, stakeholder requirements, and implementation explicit. Working across design and code made me responsible for turning a change in direction into something concrete, while staying clear about which decisions were mine.

A later employee rebuilt the broader RUHL AI website during my absence. I do not claim that subsequent website, its design, or its outcomes as my work.
