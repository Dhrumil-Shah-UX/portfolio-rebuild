Problem

A medical match is only part of the decision.

Patients also need to understand the information, manage practical constraints, and choose who participates.

Understand the trial
Unfamiliar language and fragmented details → explanation and aligned comparison.
Judge practical fit
Travel and visit requirements → preferences alongside medical context.
Control participation
Caregiver access and peer messaging → patient permissions and approval.

Research foundation Literature review, affinity mapping and recurring physician collaboration, including three think-aloud rounds. Real patients were not tested.

Decisions

Four decisions along the patient journey.

  2. Explore suggestions

01/04Onboarding

Offer record sync without requiring it.

Physicians questioned onboarding depth in rounds 1 and 3. The team explored EHR sync alongside manual entry; I owned key onboarding flows.

Suggest a possibility, not clinical certainty.

03/04Comparison

Compare the differences in one place.

I worked on side-by-side comparison, aligning timelines, visit frequency and eligibility considerations so patients could inspect differences without relying on memory.

04/04Support throughout the journey

Let patients choose who sees what.

Physicians raised unwanted messaging in round 1 and privacy concerns in later feedback. I proposed peer connection requests and worked on Care Circle interactions; the team refined caregiver sharing controls.

Patient control Caregiver permissions govern access and actions; peer requests require approval. Care Circle also explored assigned tasks.

What changed

More cautious suggestion language, clearer participation controls, and continued onboarding refinement. Suggestion accuracy remained an open concern after round 3.

What remains unmeasured

Patient comprehension, enrollment and clinical effectiveness. Delivery and testing counts describe activity, not measured product impact.

Scope judgment

A clinician dashboard and broader physician/EHR hub were explored but not adopted because they required many supporting features. Selected elements informed the final concept.

Physician collaboration taught me to balance clarity, clinical accuracy and patient control. Direct patient research would be the next step toward evaluating the experience.
